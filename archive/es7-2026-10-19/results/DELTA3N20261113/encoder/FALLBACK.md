## RAM estimate, explicitly conditional on representation

At four bytes per encoded literal, the flat formula's literal payload is
15,738,412 bytes (15.0 MiB) at m14 and 42,647,320 bytes (40.7 MiB) at m15.
These are representation estimates, not measured CaDiCaL RSS or lower
bounds on a solver that compresses some clauses.

A planning allowance of **two copies of that literal payload plus 64
bytes per clause** gives 34.4 MiB and 88.6 MiB respectively. This allowance
does not include allocator fragmentation, additional solver tables,
preprocessing, learned clauses, or the proof checker. It is not an upper
bound on a search. The existing AS1300 MiB / MemoryMax1800M reservation
remains the enforced cap; no extra worker is implicitly requested.

With variable IDs below 10,000, each printed literal needs at most six
bytes including its sign and following space. Counting `0\n` per clause
and the 96-byte header gives conservative DIMACS size bounds of
23,750,132 bytes for m14 and 64,208,174 bytes for m15. Actual files would
be smaller; these two stats calls did not write them. LRAT output has no
useful bound from those numbers and can be several GB.
