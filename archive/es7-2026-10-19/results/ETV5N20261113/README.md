## 2. Model and soundness (PROVED: written arguments; checks as stated)

Throughout, S is a finite planar point set in general position with distinct x-coordinates (rotate
if necessary), with no 5-cap and no convex octagon; |S| ≥ 2. Points are indexed by increasing x.
"Cup"/"cap" are as in math.md §2.1 (positive/negative determinant on increasing triples).

**Labels.** For u < v let E₋(u,v), E₊(u,v) be the largest number of points of a cap / cup of S
ending with the pair (u,v). Since S has no 5-cap and no 8-cup (an 8-cup is a convex octagon),
d(u,v) = (E₋(u,v) − 1, E₊(u,v) − 1) ∈ [1..3] × [1..6].

**Growth** (math.md §2.1, extension of an attaining chain): for u < v < w, if (u,v,w) is a cap then
d₁(v,w) > d₁(u,v); if it is a cup then d₂(v,w) > d₂(u,v). With the ceilings 3 and 6 this also says:
d₁(u,v) = 3 forces every later w to make a cup with (u,v), and d₂(u,v) = 6 forces a cap.

**States.** I(v) is the down-set of [1..3] × [1..6] generated by {d(u,v) : u < v}. It is encoded by
the column heights 6 ≥ f₁ ≥ f₂ ≥ f₃ ≥ 0 (column a = cap coordinate a), so there are C(9,3) = 84
states. State separation (math.md Lemma 2.1, same proof): d(u,v) ∈ I(v) \ I(u) for u < v; hence the
owner map v ↦ I(v) is injective and |S| = 84 − (number of holes).

**Fixed states.** The first point has I = ∅ (state 0). The second point has the single incoming
label (1,1) (a pair is a 2-cap and a 2-cup), so its state is f = (1,0,0) (state 1). Both are occupied
whenever |S| ≥ 2.

**Lemma R (rank attainment; the ETV analogue of math.md Lemma 6.1).** For u < v,
d₂(u,v) ≤ cupH(u) + 1 and d₁(u,v) ≤ capH(u) + 1, where cupH(u) = f₁(u) is the largest second
coordinate of a label ending at u (0 if u is the first point), and capH(u) = #{a : f_a(u) ≥ 1} is the
largest first coordinate of a label ending at u.

*Proof.* Let k = E₊(u,v), attained by a cup C ending with (u,v). If k = 2, d₂(u,v) = 1. Otherwise
u has a predecessor t on C, and C minus v is a cup of k − 1 points ending with (t,u). So
E₊(t,u) ≥ k − 1, i.e. d₂(t,u) ≥ d₂(u,v) − 1. Since d₁(t,u) ≥ 1, the cell (1, d₂(t,u)) lies in I(u),
so f₁(u) ≥ d₂(u,v) − 1. The cap statement is the same argument with E₋: the predecessor gives
d₁(t,u) ≥ d₁(u,v) − 1 and the cell (d₁(t,u), 1) ∈ I(u), so column d₁(t,u) is nonempty. ∎

Hence every actual label lies in the **refined domain**
D′(s→t) = {(a,b) ∈ I(t) \ I(s) : a ≤ capH(s) + 1, b ≤ cupH(s) + 1}, where s = I(u), t = I(v).
(Remark, checked: refining the six-order bounding-box test with D′ forces no additional triple; both
tables force exactly 49,482 ordered state triples. Lemma R matters only inside the coupled CNF.)

**Cycle rows (math.md Lemma 2.2, unchanged).** If a list of k distinct states passes the six-order
bounding-box test on every triple in list order, at most 7 of them are occupied: demand k − 7.
The test uses the plain domains I(t) \ I(s), which contain every actual label by state separation.

**Coupled rows.** For a support T = (s₀, …, s_{n−1}) of distinct states, suppose all are occupied and
let v_i be the owner of s_i. The CNF of `coupled5.py` has the atoms before(i,j) (v_i left of v_j),
cap_ge(i,j,k) / cup_ge(i,j,k) (the label of the pair, earlier owner first, has first / second
coordinate ≥ k), orientation(i,j,k) (det(p_{v_i}, p_{v_j}, p_{v_k}) > 0, sorted index order; a
permutation flips the sign by parity) and convex_four(i,j,k,l). Each clause family holds under this
interpretation:
1. *Threshold monotonicity* cap_ge(k+1) → cap_ge(k), cup_ge likewise: definitions.
2. *Order transitivity*: x-order is a strict total order.
3. *Growth*: before(a,b) ∧ before(b,c) ∧ (a,b,c) negative ∧ cap_ge(a,b,k) → cap_ge(b,c,k+1), with
   cap_ge(·,·,4) false; the cup version with positive orientation and cup_ge(·,·,7) false. This is
   Growth plus the ceilings (no 5-cap, no 8-cup).
4. *Signotope packets*: for four owners in x-order a<b<c<d the sign sequence
   ([abc],[abd],[acd],[bcd]) changes sign at most once. Proof: with α,β,γ,δ these determinants,
   the 4×4 determinant identity gives δ − γ + β − α = 0, and math.md §2.1 gives
   (c_x−b_x)β = (d_x−b_x)α + (b_x−a_x)δ and (c_x−b_x)γ = (d_x−c_x)α + (c_x−a_x)δ. The eight
   sequences with two or more sign changes are ±(+,+,−,+) (second identity), ±(+,−,+,+) and
   ±(+,−,−,+) (first identity), ±(+,−,+,−) (β = α + γ − δ). All are impossible.
5. *convex_four definition*: [bcd]·a − [acd]·b + [abd]·c − [abc]·d = 0 (in homogeneous coordinates
   (1,x,y)) is the affine dependency; four points in general position are in convex position iff its
   coefficients split 2+2 (a 3+1 split puts one point inside the triangle of the others).
6. *Complete no-octagon*: for every 8 owners, some 4 of them are not in convex position. If all 70
   quadruples were convex, no owner would lie in a triangle of three others, hence (Carathéodory) not
   in the convex hull of the others: a convex octagon.
7. *empty* (u,v): D′(s_u→s_v) = ∅ ⇒ ¬before(u,v), by Lemma 2.1 + Lemma R.
8. *domain* (u,v,a,b): (a,b) ∉ D′(s_u→s_v) ⇒ (before(u,v) → label(u,v) ≠ (a,b)), same reason.
9. *positive* (i,j,k): the six-order test on (s_i,s_j,s_k) passes ⇒ orientation positive in that
   order (Lemma 2.2's check, applied to one triple).
No 5-cap among owners is not a separate family: it follows from 3 (three growth steps exceed 3).
So if the CNF is unsatisfiable, the states of T are never all occupied: **demand-one row on T**.

**Transport (PROVED; the RES33N / math.md §5.3 argument).** For a refuted core T (index order = sorted
states), the clauses used by an LRAT refutation are traced; the state-dependent ones (families 7–9) are
shrunk while the **template CNF** = static(n) + kept clauses stays UNSAT. The clause of a kept test
depends only on its tag (indices, and (a,b) for domain tests), not on the states. For an injective
σ: indices → states, each kept test is checked on σ: positive (i,j,k) needs TT[σi,σj,σk] (then σ's CNF
has the same unit clause, because no state triple has both orientations forced: 0 such triples);
empty (u,v) needs D′(σu→σv) = ∅ (same unit clause); domain (u,v,a,b) needs (a,b) ∉ D′(σu→σv) (same
clause, or implied by σ's unit clause ¬before(u,v) when D′ is empty). Static clauses depend only on n.
So σ's CNF implies every clause of the template CNF; if the template CNF is unsatisfiable, so is σ's
CNF, and image(σ) is a demand-one row. In the stated bound, every template CNF is CERTIFIED by cake_lpr
and every transported row carries its explicit σ, re-checked by `final_verify.py`. (During the search,
shrink verdicts are plain CaDiCaL verdicts; they are not used for any claim.)

**Empirical control of the clause semantics.** `soundness_test.py` / `soundness_test2.py`: random
5-cap-free octagon-free point sets (uniform, perturbed parabolas and arcs, clusters; up to 26 points;
states read off by exact integer DP), random owner subsets of size 8–13 in random index order; every
clause of the CNF is evaluated under the exact geometric assignment. Violations: 0 (counts in §5).

## 3. Method

1. **Base rows.** Cycle rows by lazy separation exactly as in THEORY310N `cap5_omission.py` (bitmask
   list search `cyclists.py`, maximal-list extension). Reproduced: exact optimum 13 holes with 123
   rows (CP-SAT OPTIMAL in 3 s), i.e. the known N5 ≤ 71.
2. **Coupled owner CNF** (`coupled5.py`), the RES33N `coupled.py` families with the ETV parameters:
   caps 1..3, cups 1..6, no excluded label, and both rank-attainment bounds folded into the label
   domains (D′ of §2). Static clauses (all families except empty/domain/positive) are cached per
   support size.
3. **Loop** (`loop10.py`, `loop11.py`; earlier `loop5.py`–`loop9.py` differ only in search control):
   - find a vector with at most L holes (L = current level) by CP-SAT feasibility, falling back to
     L + 1 when CP-SAT is undecided; separate cycle rows and Baek block rows first (item 5);
   - probe unions of two "faces" (7-lists of occupied states passing the six-order test), sizes 10–15
     (then 14–18), then "density-grown" supports of 15–17 (stuck vectors: 17–19) states that add the
     states with the most forced triples, with CaDiCaL 2.1.3 on the coupled CNF;
   - minimise each UNSAT support to a core (incremental CaDiCaL 1.9.5 via pysat, owner selectors);
   - transport (`transport5.py`, `engine.py`): LRAT trace of the core's refutation, shrink the
     state-dependent tests, enumerate all compatible injective relabellings (40 s per core).
4. **Exact minimum.** CP-SAT decided the level up to 16 holes, then returned UNKNOWN (60–150 s); RC2
   (core-guided MaxSAT) settled 17 in 330 s. For certificates and for 18: CaDiCaL on a pseudo-Boolean CNF
   (`pbcnf5.py`, `pb_lrat.py`, `cube_lrat.py`: rows as clauses / k-totalizers, Σh ≤ budget as a sequential
   counter, both fixed states as unit clauses; brute-force self-test on 200 random instances), with LRAT
   proofs checked by cake_lpr, monolithic (budget 16) or as 32 cubes on five states (budgets 17 and 18).
   The last level was closed by a cube-driven loop (`cubecegar.py`): re-solve only the open cubes and
   refute their vectors. HiGHS was not used for any status.
5. **Baek block rows** (`blocks5.py`). For K a 2-set of cap coordinates, a family F whose pairwise
   refined domains only use cap coordinates in K has owners with no 4-cap (a 4-cap needs three strictly
   increasing cap coordinates on its consecutive pairs), so |O ∩ F| ≤ 22 by Baek's theorem (23 points
   in general position contain a 4-cap or a convex octagon; Lean: `BaekGeometry.geometric_capacity22`,
   math.md Lemma 2.3): row (F, |F| − 22). The largest such families have 28 states (K = {1,2}: {f₃ = 0};
   K = {2,3}: {f₁ = 6}), demand 6. They are separated lazily (3 rows were added).
6. **Sufficient subset**: the rows whose clauses are referenced by the LRAT proofs (traced through the
   hints back to original clauses). Only these rows are justified, and the arithmetic certificate is
   then redone on exactly this subset, so it is self-contained.
7. **Row justification** (`template_cert.py` / `template_cert2.py`, re-checked by `final_verify.py`):
   cycle rows by a stored list order; block rows by the family check; cores by their own full CNF
   (uncached builder), CaDiCaL 2.1.3 `--lrat`, cake_lpr; transported rows by an explicit σ plus the
   cake_lpr-checked template CNF. Hashes are recorded and every LRAT is deleted after the verdict.

   - Demand-2 rows exist (`card2.py`, optional owners with "at least |T| − 1 occupied"): 26 of 236
     unions of two overlapping cores (14–15 states) allow at most |T| − 2 occupied. But such a row is
     equivalent to the |T| demand-1 rows {T − x}, so it only packages demand-1 rows; it cannot kill a
     vector whose occupied set contains no refuted subset. The real bottleneck is that the occupied
     sets of the surviving 18-hole vectors contain very few refuted subsets of ≤ 17 states (probe
     yield ≈ 1 per 1,500 candidates).
   - So the next gain must come from refuting larger occupied subsets (density-grown supports of 17–22
     states; the CNF grows with C(n, 8)), or from genuinely new capacity statements on sub-families
     (Baek-type theorems for 4-cap-free sets with a cup bound, or an exact N(cap ≤ 4, cup ≤ 6)).
3. **Exact solving**: CaDiCaL on the PB CNF with cubes on the (6, f₂, 0) states, LRAT + cake_lpr, and
   template certificates (σ + one certified template CNF) for transported rows. CP-SAT and RC2 stop
   deciding at 16–17 holes here. The LRAT volume grows quickly (budget 18: 22 GB), so more cube
   variables will be needed at budget 19.
4. **Lean**: the coupled CNF has the atoms of `CoupledSemantics` with label ranges (3, 6), the domains
   refined by Lemma R (the cap analogue of math.md Lemma 6.1 is new), and the template transport of
   math.md §5.3. The arithmetic certificate is a set of LRAT proofs over a PB encoding; a Lean route
   would replace it by a checked hitting-set tree or a RUP export.
5. **Balanced half**: needs an exact solver for its 126-state cycle-row system first (CaDiCaL cubes).
