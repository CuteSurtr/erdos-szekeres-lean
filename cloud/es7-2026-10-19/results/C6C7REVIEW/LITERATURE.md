## Precisely identified sources and what they establish

1. **Jineon Baek, “On the Erdős–Tuza–Valtr Conjecture,” arXiv:2206.04260v2; European Journal of Combinatorics 124 (2025), 104085, DOI 10.1016/j.ejc.2024.104085.** **Evidence only (source inspection).** Theorem 1.6 states that `binomial(n−1,2)+2` points with distinct x-coordinates in general position contain a 4-cap or a convex n-gon. In particular, the maximum without either is 16 for n=7 and 22 for n=8. Its direct statement is not about reflection-symmetric sets. The reflection bounds require the geometric bridge described below. The arXiv page lists v2 dated 9 October 2022. [Primary text and theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04260v2); [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04260).

2. **Bernardo Subercaseaux, Ethan Mackey, Long Qian and Marijn Heule, “Automated Symmetric Constructions in Discrete Geometry,” arXiv:2506.00224v1; DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-07021-0_3.** **Evidence only (source inspection).** Section 3.2 gives the four-layer/fourfold 16-point layer argument. Section 6.1 reports the threefold 16-point hexagon-free formula UNSAT and gives fourfold/fivefold 16-point constructions. Section 6.2 uses the 32-point orbit count and states the fourfold 32-point exclusion after reducing to six unrealizable inner 12-point patterns. Section 7 lists a twofold 32-point realization as a future task. The arXiv record still lists only v1 (30 May 2025). [Primary arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00224v1); [publisher record](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-07021-0_3). The publisher calls the volume CICM 2025 but supplies a 2026 chapter citation; do not conflate conference year with publication year. Full chapter text was not accessible; the abstract, notes and bibliography were accessible.

3. **Bogdan Dumitru, “Notes on the 33-point Erdős–Szekeres problem,” arXiv:2512.24061v1 (30 December 2025).** **Evidence only (reported computation).** Section 7, Table 1 records an UNSAT run for six pentagonal layers with three leftover points, baseline, approximately 259,000 seconds. It also lists eleven triangular layers, but marks its sub-cube parameter unrecorded. Section 5.2 explicitly describes optional anchoring restrictions. The pentagonal baseline is the relevant antecedent to the fivefold ES(7) implication; this review did not replay that run or locate a retained proof. The arXiv record still lists only v1. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24061v1); [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24061).

## Elementary implications checked in this review

**Written proof (reviewed here, not refereed): reflection component of Theorem A.** Put the mirror axis on the x-axis and write `S=R ∪ mirror(R) ∪ A`, with R above the axis and at most two axis points A. Equal x-coordinates among two points of R would produce a collinear triple with a reflected point, so R has distinct x-coordinates. A 4-cap above the axis and its reflection give eight points in convex position. A 4-cap in `R ∪ {a}` for an axis point a has a as an endpoint and produces seven convex vertices together with its reflection. Thus a heptagon-free S has no such cap. Applying Baek at n=7 gives `|R|≤16`, or `|R|≤15` when A is nonempty; hence `|S|≤32`. This is an inference combining the published theorem with the mirror argument, not a quotation of a mirror theorem from Baek.

**Written proof (reviewed here, not refereed): pentagonal baseline implication.** A fivefold 35-point heptagon-free set has seven pentagonal layers, by the layer lemma. Deleting two points from the innermost layer gives six full pentagonal hull layers and three inner points, within the baseline family reported by Dumitru. Consequently, a sound UNSAT certificate for that family would imply the fivefold exclusion. The implication is mathematical; the external run remains a reported computational verdict in this review.

**Written proof (reviewed here, not refereed): orbit-counting rule.** A rotation of exact order `m≥2` fixes only its centre; all other orbits have size m. Therefore a finite invariant set has `mt+ε` points, `ε∈{0,1}`. For `2^(m−1)` points and even m, parity forces ε=0 and m divides a power of two, so m is a power of two. For odd m>1, m cannot divide the power of two, so ε=1 and `2^(m−1)≡1 (mod m)`: an odd prime or an odd composite Fermat pseudoprime to base 2. Conversely these congruences permit the requisite number of orbits; generic radii and phases yield general position, with the centre only in the odd case. None of this proves that such sets avoid a convex `(m+1)`-gon.
