# SAT confirmation: N(7,4,7) <= 16 (no 4-cap and no convex 7-gon forces at most 16 points)

Why: by Theorem A(ii) of `../PROOFS.md`, N_mirror(7) <= 2 N(7,4,7). Baek (Eur. J. Combin. 124 (2025) 104085,
arXiv:2206.04260, Theorem 1.6 with n = 7) gives N(7,4,7) = 16 by a written proof, and Balko–Valtr (ENDM 49, 2015)
reported N(4,7,7) = 16 by SAT in their notation. This certificate checks the same instance independently, so
N_mirror(7) <= 32 also follows from a 5 MB certificate. The note's proof of the same mirror bound uses the 33- and
34-point mirror certificates (`WRITEUP/MIRROR.md`, `SYMZ`).

## Encoding (`enc.py`, sha256 0aed2c2177d8a99fd7ade35e516d4b4c2de9a51a240fe87f45087456449f65f9)

Points 0..n-1 in x-order. t(i,j,k) (i<j<k) means orient(p_i,p_j,p_k) > 0. B(l,i,j,e) means "an l-cup starts with
p_i, p_j and ends at p_e" (3 <= l <= K-1). The clause families are (SIG), (CAP4), (B3), (BL), (KCUP) and (UP), as in the
docstring of `enc.py`. No symmetry breaking. The cup variables are indexed by the first two points and the end point;
the encoding was written independently of the other encoders of the project.

**Soundness.** Let p_0..p_{n-1} be a real x-sorted set in general position with no 4-cap and no 7 points in convex
position. Set t to the real orientations and B to its intended meaning. Every clause holds:

* (SIG) For i<j<k<l let T = (t(ijk), t(ijl), t(ikl), t(jkl)). Every 3-subsequence of T consists of three triples with a
  common point c. By Lemma 2.1 of `../PROOFS.md` their signs are sign(β-α), sign(γ-α), sign(γ-β) for three slopes α, β, γ
  of segments through c:
  * (ijk,ijl,ikl), c = i: s_ik - s_ij, s_il - s_ij, s_il - s_ik;
  * (ijl,ikl,jkl), c = l: s_jl - s_il, s_kl - s_il, s_kl - s_jl;
  * (ijk,ijl,jkl), c = j: s_jk - s_ij, s_jl - s_ij, s_jl - s_jk;
  * (ijk,ikl,jkl), c = k: s_jk - s_ik, s_kl - s_ik, s_kl - s_jk.
  In each line the middle difference is the sum of the outer two. So if the outer two signs equal e, the middle one is e
  too, and the patterns +,-,+ and -,+,- cannot occur. These are exactly the excluded patterns.
* (CAP4) A 4-cap i,j,k,l is exactly "t(ijk) and t(jkl) both false".
* (B3), (BL) If t(i,j,m) holds and an (l-1)-cup starts with j, m and ends at e, then i, j, m, ..., e is an l-cup.
* (KCUP) Otherwise i followed by a 6-cup starting with j, m would be a 7-cup, which is 7 points in convex position.
* (UP) If a 6-cup C runs from p_i to p_e and some i < p < e has t(i,p,e) false (p above the chord), then p ∉ C, since
  interior cup points are below the chord. Then C ∪ {p} is in convex position by Lemma 2.3 of `../PROOFS.md` (cap
  (i,p,e), cup C): 7 points, which is excluded.

So the CNF for n is satisfiable whenever such a real set of n points exists. UNSAT for n = 17 gives N(7,4,7) <= 16.
Points of convex position other than the ones encoded do not need to be excluded for soundness. Completeness (the
converse) is only used to read SAT answers: with no 4-cap, a convex 7-gon is a 7-cup or a 6-cup plus a point above its
chord (Lemma 2.3 with |U| <= 3).

A review found that the (SIG) clauses are not needed: without them the n = 17 formula is still UNSAT, with an LRAT proof
accepted by lrat-check. This agrees with Baek's Theorem 2.7, which holds for arbitrary 2-colourings of triples.
