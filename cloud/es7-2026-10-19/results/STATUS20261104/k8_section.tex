\section{Rotational symmetry for \texorpdfstring{$k=8$}{k=8}: upper bounds}\label{sec:k8}

Let $\rho$ be the rotation by $2\pi/m$ about $O$. For $m\ge3$ and $K\le 2m$ let $L(m,K)$ be the largest $t$ for which
there are $t$ nested regular $m$-gons centred at $O$ whose union is in general position and has no $K$ points in convex
position. By Lemma~\ref{k8:layer} below, an $m$-fold symmetric set in general position with no convex $K$-gon has at most
$mL(m,K)+1$ points, and at most $mL(m,K)$ if $m$ is even. The lower bounds $L(7,8)\ge9$ and $L(6,8)\ge10$ come from
explicit sets (exact computation): seven order types of sevenfold symmetric $64$-point sets (nine heptagons and the
centre) and five order types of sixfold symmetric $60$-point sets (ten
hexagons)~\cite[\dirn{ES8SYM}, \dirn{PRIMEFOLD}, \dirn{SIX60}]{Supp}. This section proves the matching upper bounds for
$m=7$ and $m=6$ (Table~\ref{k8:tab:status}).

Every statement carries one of the labels Lean-checked; certificate; exact computation; written proof
(not refereed); evidence only. A \emph{certificate} is an LRAT proof of unsatisfiability of a CNF formula accepted by
the verified checker cake\_lpr (its line \texttt{s VERIFIED UNSAT}); neither the solver (CaDiCaL~3.0.1) nor lrat-check
is trusted. The lrat-check of drat-trim at commit \texttt{2e3b2dc} accepts an invalid proof of a satisfiable formula
unless patched, a publicly known defect (drat-trim issues \#31 and \#42, pull request \#43; \dirn{LRATCHECK20261105}), so
its verdicts below are supporting only. A certificate proves a
geometric statement only together with the soundness of the encoding, whose status is stated separately
(\S\ref{k8:sec:trust}). Paths refer to the supplementary directories~\cite{Supp}.

\subsection{The layer lemma and the encoding facts in Lean}\label{k8:sec:lean}

\begin{lemma}[layer lemma; Lean-checked (\dirn{LAYERLEAN})]\label{k8:layer}
Let $m\ge3$, $K\le2m$, and let $S$ be a finite $\rho$-invariant set in general position with no $K$ points in
convex position. Then $S\setminus\{O\}$ is the union of regular $m$-gons $L_0,L_1,\dots,L_{t-1}$ centred at $O$ (its
convex layers), and for $n<n'$, every vertex $x$ of $L_n$ and every $y\in L_{n'}$ satisfy $\orient(x,\rho x,y)>0$. If $m$
is even and $S\ne\{O\}$, then $O\notin S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The set of hull vertices of $S\setminus\{O\}$ is $\rho$-invariant and nonempty, so it is a union of orbits of exactly
$m$ points each; two orbits would give $2m\ge K$ points in convex position, so it is one orbit, a regular $m$-gon. Peel
it off and repeat. For strict nesting, with $\tau=\pi/m$,
\[
\orient(x,\rho x,\rho^j x)=4\,|x-O|^2\sin\tau\,\sin j\tau\,\sin(j-1)\tau\ \ge0,
\]
so the vertices of a layer, and by convexity its hull, lie weakly to the left of each edge; equality would give three
collinear points of $S$. For even $m$, $\rho^{m/2}$ is the half-turn, and $p,O,2O-p$ are collinear.
\end{proof}

In \dirn{LAYERLEAN/LayerLemma.lean} (Lean~4.24.0, Mathlib \texttt{f897ebcf}; axioms \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound} only) the core statement \lean{hullVerts_eq_orbitFS} is proved for an
injective affine map with all minimal periods equal to $m$, with no general position assumed; the layers are
\lean{layers_are_orbits} and \lean{exists_layer}, strict nesting is \lean{strict_nesting} (it needs general position and
$m\ge2$), and the centre is \lean{centre_not_mem_of_even}. The written version is \dirn{SYM5/README.md}~\S2.
SMQH~\cite[\S3.2]{SMQH25} use the special case of $16$ points with fourfold symmetry and no convex hexagon (four layers
of four points).

\paragraph{Polar form} (Lean-checked, \lean{orient_polar}). Write the points of the layers as $O+r(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. Then
\[
\orient(a,b,d)=r_ar_b\sin(\theta_b-\theta_a)+r_br_d\sin(\theta_d-\theta_b)+r_dr_a\sin(\theta_a-\theta_d).
\]

\begin{lemma}[encoding facts; Lean-checked (\dirn{LAYERLEAN})]\label{k8:facts}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{k8:layer}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item\emph{(rule A)} if the three angular gaps from $a$ to $b$ to $d$ lie in $(0,\pi)$, so that $O$ lies inside the
triangle, then $\orient(a,b,d)>0$;
\item\emph{(rule B)} if $\theta_b=\theta_a+x$ and $\theta_d=\theta_b+y$ with $x,y>0$, $x+y<\pi$, and $r_b\ge
r_a,r_d$, then $\orient(a,b,d)>0$;
\item\emph{(radius order)} points of later layers are no farther from $O$ than points of earlier layers;
\item\emph{(chains)} if $K\ge4$, there are no pivot $p\in L_i$ and points $q_0,\dots,q_{K-2}$ of
$L_i\cup L_{i+1}\cup\cdots$, all other than $p$, with $\orient(p,q_s,q_{s+1})>0$ and $\orient(q_s,q_{s+1},q_{s+2})>0$
for all admissible $s$;
\item\emph{(reduction)} if every $\rho$-invariant set in general position with exactly $mt$ points and without $O$ has
a convex $K$-gon, then so does every $\rho$-invariant set in general position with more than $m(t-1)$ points other than
$O$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a)~All three sines are positive. (b)~By $r_b\ge r_a,r_d$ the polar form is at least
$r_ar_d(\sin x+\sin y-\sin(x+y))>0$. (c)~By convexity of the disc and Lemma~\ref{k8:layer}. (d)~\emph{Fan lemma:} a
pivot and $n\ge3$ chain points in an open half-plane through the pivot, with all these turns positive, are in convex
position. \emph{Half-plane:} every other point of the layers $\ge i$ lies strictly on the inner side of the tangent line
at $p$; for $\rho^jp$ the value is $|p-O|^2(1-\cos(2\pi j/m))$, and for later layers it follows from strict nesting at
the two edges through $p$. So a chain would be a convex $K$-gon. (e)~Peel $t$ layers of exactly $m$ points.
\end{proof}

The Lean names are \lean{orient_polar}, \lean{orient_polar_centre_inside}, \lean{orient_polar_halfplane} (using
$\sin x+\sin y>\sin(x+y)$, \lean{sin_add_lt}), \lean{dist2_le_of_later}, \lean{chain_clauses_sound} (with
\lean{hasConvexNGon_of_fan} and \lean{lin_pos_of_peel}) and \lean{convexNGon_of_layers}.

\subsection{The encoding}\label{k8:sec:enc}

Fix $t$ layers and rotate so that layer $0$ has a vertex at angle $0$. The encoder \dirn{C7UPPER/gen.py} (encoder A) has
one variable per rotation orbit of ordered triples (the orientation), phase-order variables $Y(i,j)=[\psi_i<\psi_j]$
with $\psi=\theta\bmod\pi/m$ for odd $m$ and $\psi=\theta\bmod 2\pi/m$ for even $m$, variables $H(i)=[\theta_i<\pi/m]$ for
odd $m$, and auxiliary chain variables. Its clauses are:
\begin{itemize}
\item\emph{angular rules:} for each triple, the circular order of its directions and their antipodes is determined by
the phase order of its layers, and where rule A or rule B applies the orientation is fixed accordingly;
\item\emph{nesting:} $\orient(x,\rho x,y)>0$ for $x$ in a layer and $y$ in a later one (Lemma~\ref{k8:layer});
\item\emph{chains:} no chain of $K-1$ points around a pivot $(i,0)$ as in Lemma~\ref{k8:facts}(d). By rotation this
covers every convex $K$-gon: take its outermost layer as $i$.
\end{itemize}
Even $m$ needs no $H$ variables, since antipodes are orbit points; a triple containing an antipodal pair of one layer
gets no angular clause, which is weaker but sound. The encoder \dirn{C7UPPER/gen\_b.py}, extended to even $m$ as
\dirn{C6UPPER/encb/gen\_b2.py} (encoder B), implements the same families separately: exact integer circular orders
instead of sampled float patterns, chains built backwards, a different variable order. The two encoders share the
written soundness argument, the canonical triple representatives and part of the validation code, so they are separate
implementations of one encoding, not independent encodings.

\emph{Soundness} (every real configuration of $t$ nested regular $m$-gons in general position without a convex $K$-gon
satisfies the formula) is the docstring proof of \dirn{gen.py}: written proof (not refereed), reviewed line by line for
$(m,K)=(4,8),(5,8),(6,8)$ (\dirn{ROT456}~\S2) and again for $m=6,7$ (\dirn{C6C7REVIEW}), with no blocking gap found. The
parts in Lemmas~\ref{k8:layer} and~\ref{k8:facts} are Lean-checked; the rest is listed in \S\ref{k8:sec:trust}.

\emph{Validation} (evidence only, except where stated). Fresh runs reproduce every formula byte for byte. The known
sevenfold $63$- and $64$-point sets satisfy every clause of the $9$-layer formula, and the $54$-point sixfold set and
its mirror image satisfy every clause of both encoders' $9$-layer formulas (exact computation, integer arithmetic in
the coordinates of the sixfold cyclotomic weights, \dirn{C6C7REVIEW}). On random configurations, chain clauses agree with
brute force over all $8$-subsets, and the non-chain clauses of A and B agree on all $185{,}549$ ($m=7$) and $35{,}590$
($m=6$) orbit-pattern pairs compared. Calibration: for $(m,K)=(3,4),(4,5),(5,6),(6,7)$ the encoding is satisfiable
exactly at the known maximum number of layers and unsatisfiable one layer beyond (proofs first accepted by lrat-check;
certified by cake\_lpr on 5 November, \dirn{LRATCHECK20261105/recheck}).

\subsection{Sevenfold symmetry}

\begin{theorem}[certificate, two encoders; reduction Lean-checked; lower bound exact computation]
\label{k8:c7}
Every configuration of $10$ nested regular heptagons about a common centre, in general position and without the centre,
contains $8$ points in convex position. Hence $L(7,8)=9$: every sevenfold rotationally symmetric set of at least $65$
points in general position contains a convex octagon, and the largest sevenfold symmetric set in general position
without a convex octagon has exactly $64$ points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\emph{Reduction} (Lean-checked, \lean{sevenfold_octagon}). By Lemma~\ref{k8:facts}(e), the statement follows from
\lean{Sevenfold70}: every centre-free sevenfold set of $70$ points in general position has a convex octagon. A set of
at least $65$ points has at least $10$ layers ($7\cdot9+1=64$); lowering $65$ to $64$ makes the Lean proof fail.

\emph{Certificate.} Both encoders give \texttt{p cnf 80080 2748538} at $10$ layers. Encoder A adds $\theta_0=0$ and the
unit $H(1)$, which loses nothing: the reflection in the $x$-axis keeps $\theta_0=0$ and swaps $H(1)$. Encoder B uses
only $\theta_0=0$. CaDiCaL~3.0.1 reports UNSAT in $833$\,s and $802$\,s; the LRAT proofs ($1{,}702{,}278{,}368$ and
$1{,}630{,}259{,}870$ bytes) are accepted by lrat-check and by cake\_lpr, and both were replayed against freshly generated
formulas with both checkers on 4 November (\dirn{C6C7REVIEW}). Hashes are in \dirn{C7UPPER/SHA256SUMS.txt}.

\emph{Lower bound} (exact computation). The sevenfold $64$-point sets above.
\end{proof}

Sevenfold affine symmetries reduce to rotations by the averaging argument of \dirn{SYMLEAN}. The $64$-point sets have a
$k=6$ analogue in print, the fivefold $16$-point set with centre of~\cite[App.~C, Table~2]{SMQH25}
(also~\cite[Fig.~3.33]{Mac25}).

\subsection{Sixfold symmetry}

\begin{theorem}[certificate, two encoders (for what was replayed see the proof), on top of a written proof (not
refereed) of the reflection unit; reduction Lean-checked; lower bound exact computation]\label{k8:c6}
Every configuration of $11$ nested regular hexagons about a common centre, in general position, contains $8$ points in
convex position. Hence $L(6,8)=10$: every sixfold rotationally symmetric set of at least $61$ points in general position
contains a convex octagon, and the largest sixfold symmetric set in general position without a convex octagon has
exactly $60$ points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\emph{Reduction} (Lean-checked, \lean{sixfold_octagon}). The statement follows from \lean{Sixfold66}. For even $m$ the
centre is excluded (Lemma~\ref{k8:layer}), so sizes are multiples of $6$ and $61$ points need $11$ layers.

\emph{Symmetry breaking} (written proof (not refereed), \dirn{C6UPPER}~\S2). For even $m$ and $i\ge1$, general position
gives distinct phases $\psi_i\in(0,2\pi/m)$. The reflection $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$ keeps the layers, their radii and the
vertex at angle $0$, preserves general and convex position, and maps $\psi_i$ to $2\pi/m-\psi_i$. It reverses the phase
order of layers $1,\dots,t-1$, so one of the two configurations satisfies $Y(1,2)$. The $54$-point set and its mirror
image satisfy the $9$-layer formulas with $Y(1,2)$ false and true respectively (exact computation).

\emph{Split.} The base formula (encoder A: $78{,}059$ variables, $2{,}387{,}083$ clauses) plus $Y(1,2)$ is split into
all $32$ sign patterns of $Y(1,3),Y(1,4),Y(2,3),Y(2,4),Y(3,4)$. A standard-library checker confirms that each cube file
is the base formula byte for byte plus its units and that the patterns are distinct, and it rejects mutated covers
(exact computation). Twenty patterns contradict transitivity and are refuted at once; the other twelve are the orders of
$\psi_1,\dots,\psi_4$ with $\psi_1<\psi_2$.

\emph{Certificates.} For encoder A the twelve orders took $1{,}230$--$2{,}068$\,s each ($41.0$\,GB of proofs); for
encoder B, with the same split on its own formula, $1{,}495$--$2{,}553$\,s ($45.7$\,GB). All $64$ proofs were accepted
by lrat-check and by cake\_lpr when solved (3 November); the cake\_lpr logs record cube labels, not formula or proof
hashes. On 4 November three randomly chosen phase-order proofs per encoder were replayed with both checkers against
freshly generated formulas, with matching hashes (\dirn{C6C7REVIEW}), so $6$ of the $64$ cake\_lpr verdicts are bound
to hashes; the same day all $64$ proofs, with hashes matching the recorded ones, were replayed against freshly generated
formulas, but with lrat-check only, which is not a certificate. The other $58$ cake\_lpr verdicts are as recorded at solve time; a full
hash-bound cake\_lpr replay is planned. An unsplit run was stopped after $6{,}578$\,s without an answer; it proves
nothing.

\emph{Lower bound} (exact computation). The sixfold $60$-point sets A, B, C, C$'$ and D of \dirn{SIX60/sets} (ten
hexagons, coordinates in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_6]$; with $296{,}544$, $301{,}674$, $221{,}976$, $223{,}416$ and $316{,}182$
convex $7$-subsets they are of pairwise different order types), each checked by at least three independently written
programs (\dirn{SIX60}). The earlier $54$-point set \dirn{ES8SYM/sets/c6\_54pts\_9layers} is no longer needed for the lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the former gap $54$ versus $60$]\label{k8:gap}
The plain encoding is satisfiable at $10$ layers. Adding the $3$-term Grassmann--Pl\"ucker sign relations lazily gives an
abstract sixfold $60$-point model that satisfies all $27{,}307{,}560$ of them and has no $7$-chain; it has not been
realized (evidence only: the best numerical fit leaves $249$ of $5{,}710$ orbit signs wrong) (\dirn{ROT456}~\S3). The reviews exclude such models one at a time for the prescribed sixfold rotation (written proof
(not refereed), the identities verified by exact computation): the archived $60$-point model by a three-comparison
determinant-product cycle (\dirn{C6C7REVIEW}), and, of $40$ fully GP-consistent models of a later refinement, $36$ by
linear identities, $2$ by directed product cycles and $1$ by a negative-definite quadratic form; the last one is
unresolved (\dirn{ROTREFINE20261104}). These exclusions concern
the geometric sixfold action only, not arbitrary realizations of the order types. The question was settled by
construction: the $60$-point sets of \dirn{SIX60} exist. The orientation signs of set A extend to a model of encoder A's
$10$-layer formula and satisfy every clause of the cut families produced up to 5 November (exact computation,
\dirn{SIX60}); since sound cuts cannot exclude a real configuration, its cube could never have become unsatisfiable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Trust base}\label{k8:sec:trust}

\begin{itemize}
\item\emph{Lean-checked:} Lemma~\ref{k8:layer} with strict nesting and the even-$m$ centre exclusion;
Lemma~\ref{k8:facts} (the polar form and rules A and B, the radius order, the soundness of the chain family, the
reduction to exactly $t$ layers); the deductions of Theorems~\ref{k8:c7} and~\ref{k8:c6} from \lean{Sevenfold70} and
\lean{Sixfold66}.
\item\emph{Certificate:} the unsatisfiability of the two sevenfold formulas (cake\_lpr, both replayed against
regenerated formulas with matching hashes) and of the $64$ sixfold cube formulas (cake\_lpr at solve time; $6$ of the
$64$ replayed hash-bound, see the proof of Theorem~\ref{k8:c6}). Trusted: cake\_lpr.
\item\emph{Exact computation:} the cube cover; the lower-bound sets; the real-set tests of the sixfold formulas.
\item\emph{Written proof (not refereed), reviewed:} (i)~the classification of phase patterns, i.e.\ which of rules A and
B applies, and to which ordered triple, as a function of the discrete phase pattern (exact integer comparisons in
encoder B, float sampling in encoder A); (ii)~the perturbation to generic phases and the consistency of one variable per
triple orbit (for even $m$ a triple stabiliser can only act as a $3$-cycle); (iii)~the code-to-clause correspondence,
i.e.\ that the generators emit exactly these families; (iv)~the reflection unit for $m=6$.
\item\emph{Evidence only:} the random, brute-force and cross-encoder tests.
\end{itemize}
No step from a CNF formula to the Lean propositions \lean{Sevenfold70} and \lean{Sixfold66} is formalized. A review of
4 November found no blocking gap; some comments in \dirn{C7UPPER/gen\_b.py} and the angular docstring of
\dirn{C7UPPER/gen.py} still state chain implications in the reverse direction (documentation only; the emitted formulas
are unchanged).

\subsection{The open threefold, fourfold and fivefold cases}

Sizes needed to violate $ES(8)=65$ are $13$ layers ($m=5$, with or without centre), $17$ layers ($m=4$) and $22$ orbits
without centre ($m=3$). Nothing below is a theorem; timed-out runs prove nothing.

\emph{Fivefold} (evidence only). With the unit $H(1)$ and the $64$ sign patterns of $Y(1,2),\dots,Y(3,4)$, the $40$
impossible patterns are refuted at once (lrat-check). Five of the $24$ phase orders ran $3{,}059$--$3{,}257$\,s each
without an answer; splitting one of them further by layer $5$ left pieces that ran $6{,}570$\,s each without an answer;
two other phase-order cubes ran $22{,}102$\,s each, about $5\cdot10^7$ conflicts, without an answer, against
$2.0$--$2.6\cdot10^6$ conflicts for the sixfold cubes (\dirn{ROT456}~\S4). The plain encoding is satisfiable at $12$
layers. Four exact linear identities over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ were found and tested on the $56$-point set; one of them
excludes a fully GP-consistent abstract $60$-point model (written proof (not refereed), verified by exact
computation), but a strengthened $13$-layer formula still gave no answer in
$600$\,s (\dirn{ROTREFINE20261104}). Threshold runs of 5 November with added chord-lemma clauses (\dirn{CHORD20261105},
\dirn{ROT45CHORD20261105}) refuted the $40$ impossible patterns again, with proofs accepted by cake\_lpr, but closed none
of the $24$ phase orders: $19$ of them ran for up to about $15$ hours, and $5$ were never started.

\emph{Fourfold} (evidence only). One run of the $17$-layer formula stopped at $1{,}500$\,s without an answer; the
5 November runs with chord-lemma clauses closed none of the $12$ phase orders in about $15$ hours (one run was lost;
\dirn{ROT45CHORD20261105}). At $14$
layers a fully GP-consistent abstract $56$-point model exists and has not been realized (best fit: $424$ of $6{,}930$
orbit signs wrong).

\emph{Threefold.} Here $8>2\cdot3$, so Lemma~\ref{k8:layer} does not apply. Every convex layer is a triangle, a
hexagon or the centre (written proof (not refereed), \dirn{ES8SYM}~\S3), and since $3\cdot21+1<65$, a threefold
counterexample to $ES(8)=65$ exists if and only if some threefold set of $22$ orbits without centre is octagon-free.
Near misses (exact computation): a threefold $64$-point set and its $63$-point subset without the centre, each with
exactly three convex octagons. A radius-ordered relaxation that omits the nesting clauses has a written soundness
argument (\dirn{C6C7REVIEW}); it has not been implemented or tested.
