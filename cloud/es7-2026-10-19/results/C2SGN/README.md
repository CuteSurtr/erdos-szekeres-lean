# C2SGN: a compact encoding for the parallelogram case of C2C34 (experimental)

Local runs on an Apple M5 Pro, 2026-10-29 (UTC). **No theorem is claimed here.**

This directory records:
- a second encoding of the open part of `results/C2C34`;
- its tests and its independent review;
- certified pieces and measured costs.

**The open part.** A centrally symmetric 34-point set in general position without a convex 7-gon would have a
parallelogram hull (`results/C2C34`). The encoding used there (`results/SYMZ/zcnf.py`):
- has 838,032 variables and 16.6M clauses;
- needs up to 8 GB per CaDiCaL process;
- splits the parallelogram case into 8 canonical cases P_a.

## 1. Encoding (`sgn.py`)

- **Labelling.** Points 1..n are sorted by a direction d along which all points have distinct coordinates.
  - o(i,j,k), for i<j<k, is the orientation of (p_i, p_j, p_k).
- **Signotope axioms.** For i<j<k<l, the sequence o(ijk), o(ijl), o(ikl), o(jkl) changes sign at most once.
- **Central symmetry** (`--sym`). p_{n+1-i} = -p_i, so o(n+1-k, n+1-j, n+1-i) = -o(i,j,k). There is one variable per
  orbit.
- **No convex 7-gon.** A set sorted by x is in convex position iff it is an s-cup and a t-cap with the same first and
  last point, s + t = 9.
  - Cup variables U_k(a,j,l) are forced by U_{k-1}(a,i,j) and o(i,j,l).
  - A cap from a to b is the image of a cup from n+1-b to n+1-a.
- **Centre** (`--centre`). The centre c lies between p_{n/2} and p_{n/2+1} in x-order.
  - It satisfies chi(c,p,q) = chi(-p,p,q).
  - The signotope axioms are added for c with any three points containing no antipodal pair.
- **Parallelogram hull** (`--par`). d is the outward normal of a hull edge V1 -> V2 (counter-clockwise), tilted towards
  V2.
  - Then p_n = V2, p_{n-1} = V1, p_1 = -V2 and p_2 = -V1, and the hull is p_{n-1} -> p_n -> p_2 -> p_1.
  - Unit clauses put every other point strictly left of each hull edge.
- **Edge-pair choice** (`--rs`). The diagonals split the 15 inner pairs into the class of edge V1V2 and the class of
  edge V2(-V1). The edge pair with the larger class is used, so at least 8 pairs are in the class of V1V2.
- **The formula.** `sgn.py 34 7 --sym --par --centre --rs`:
  - 30,412 variables and 399,739 clauses (7.2 MB);
  - SHA-256 `3459d4c5ba339ab07ce41eeb54a7f8a6582991584da6da56e7b6eb4c1708f9d1`, regenerated byte-identically;
  - 0.1–0.3 GB per CaDiCaL process;
  - one formula for every parallelogram hull.
- **Calibration options.** `--hexk k` (hexagonal hull) and `--mirror I J K` (one fixed inner orientation) are used only
  for calibration.

## 2. Splits

- **Second layer** (`l2sgn.py`).
  - **The layer.** The 30 inner points have a centrally symmetric hull with 4 or 6 vertices; 8 would be a convex
    7-gon.
  - **The cubes.** A cube fixes the vertex pairs and their counter-clockwise order, and puts every other inner point
    strictly left of each edge. There are 3,850 cubes.
  - **After unit propagation** (`tools/upfilter.c`), 392 remain: 28 quadrilaterals and 364 hexagons
    (`logs/second_layer_up_filter.txt`). All contain pair 3, since the leftmost inner point is a vertex of the inner hull.
  - **Cover.** `camp/cover_ok.cnf` is the formula plus one clause per remaining cube.
- **Mirror orbits.**
  - **The argument.** Reflect a real configuration and label it again. With 15 inner pairs (odd), the `--rs` edge pair is
    unique and unchanged.
    - After a perturbation that keeps the order type, no two inner points are equally far from the chosen edge. The
      new labelling then swaps 1<->2 and n-1<->n and negates every inner orientation.
  - **Effect on cubes.** Q3.j <-> Q3.(35-j) and H3.a.b <-> H3.(35-b).(35-a).
    - The 392 cubes form 196 orbits (14 quadrilateral, 182 hexagonal); none is fixed by the reflection
      (`camp/orbits_l2.tsv`).
    - Only representatives are refuted (`camp/reps_*.txt`).
- **Deeper layers** (`l3sgn.py`). The same construction applies inside the current layer: the leftmost remaining point
  is a vertex of the next layer. Under each Q3.j, 288 third-layer cubes survive unit propagation.
- **Adaptive closing** (`closesgn.py`).
  - A node is solved with a short cap.
  - On a timeout it is split at its next layer.
  - The split is certified by a cover proof (node cube as units, one clause per child).
  - The children are queued.
  - A node is closed when its own refutation, or its cover and all its children, are accepted by both checkers
    (`treesum.py`).

## 3. Tests and review

**Real-set tests.** Formula plus orientation units must be SAT iff the set has no convex K-gon, against brute force over
K-subsets with an exact hull (`logs/`):
- `val_*.log`:
  - random symmetric integer sets, with and without `--par`, `--centre`, `--rs`;
  - K = largest convex subset or one more;
  - 0 mismatches.
- `val_hex_centre.log`: `--hexk`, 0 mismatches.
- `realsub_k7.log`, `realsub_k9.log`:
  - 100 antipodal subsets of the 7-gon-free 32-point set of `results/SYM2`, all SAT;
  - 30 subsets of its 9-gon-free 128-point set, all SAT.
- `realtest_32_k7*.log`: the 32-point set itself (parallelogram hull), SAT with and without `--par`, `--centre`.
- `mirrortest.log`: the mirror relabelling on 300 random sets with an odd number of inner pairs, 0 violations.
  - With an even number, ties in `--rs` break it; this cannot happen at 34 points.

**Independent adversarial review.** See `REVIEW.md`; the reviewer's scripts are in `review/`.
- The review found the encoding, `--rs`, the mirror reduction (given the perturbation step) and the cube literals
  sound.
- Its conditions are met:
  - only cube-level orbit selection is used, not the `--mirror` unit;
  - the covers and all cube refutations use the same base formula.

## 4. Certified runs (check and delete)

**Method.**
- CaDiCaL 3.0.1 `--lrat`, then lrat-check and cake_lpr (heap 3,000 MB, one cake_lpr at a time).
- Each record holds the proof's size and SHA-256 and both verdicts (`logs/units.tsv`, tree `results.jsonl`).
- Proofs are deleted after both checkers accept them.
- Lanes: `camp/lane.sh` (second-layer representatives, third layer under Q3.11), `camp/covercheck.sh` (covers),
  `closesgn.py` (adaptive trees).

## 5. Exploratory runs (no proofs)

`logs/exploratory_runs.tsv` lists every exploratory run with its cap and time.
- **Lookahead cubes of depth 10** (`tools/lacube.c`, `tools/lacube2.c`): 3/3 gave no answer in 900 s.
- **Hexagonal-hull calibration** (`--hexk 10`, `--hexk 17`): no answer in 1,800 s each.
  - The zcnf hexagonal-hull cases took a median of 687 s each, over 120 cases.
- **Kissat 4.0.4 vs CaDiCaL** on two second-layer cubes: about 1.4-1.7 times faster.
  - Kissat does not write LRAT, so certified runs use CaDiCaL.
- **Nested parallelograms at 30 and 32 points:**
  - Every surviving quadrilateral second-layer cube is SAT within a second.
  - So in this abstract model, no reduction from 34 to fewer points is available for nested parallelograms. Deleting
    an inner pair keeps both outer layers.
  - Whether those abstract solutions are realizable was not checked.

## 6. Findings and estimates

**Nested parallelograms are the hard core here too.**
- **Second layer.** 8 of the 18 hexagonal representatives run were refuted within 1,800 s (45 to 1,777 s). The
  quadrilateral representatives Q3.11 and Q3.18 gave no answer in 1,200 s (exploratory runs).
- **Third layer under Q3.11.** The 288 children that survive unit propagation are covered by a certified split.
  - Hexagonal third layers: 24 of 31 run were refuted within 1,200 s (median 44 s).
  - Quadrilateral third layers: 0 of 4 run were refuted within 1,200 s.
- **Fourth layer.** Q3.11/Q4.30 was split adaptively with a 120 s cap per node.
  - 30 of its first 34 nodes gave no answer and 29 were split; each split's cover proof was accepted by both checkers.
  - 3,511 nodes were still queued when the run stopped, after about 1.3 core-hours.

**Splitting closes hard hexagonal nodes cheaply.** Q3.11/H4.10.29 gave no answer in 1,200 s as one formula. The adaptive
closer refuted it:
- 454 node runs (median 2.6 s, max 102 s) and 4 certified splits;
- about 1.0 core-hour, including both checks;
- 8.2 GB of proofs, all accepted by both checkers and deleted.

So 25 of the 288 third-layer children of Q3.11 are refuted.

**Costs compared with `results/C2C34`.**
- **Memory per CaDiCaL process.** 0.1–0.3 GB here, against 3.0–11.6 GB (median 10.1 GB) for the P8 subcases of
  `results/C2C34` (§6 there).
- **Checking.** cake_lpr runs with a 3,000 MB heap here, against 8,000 MB there. The largest proof here, the cover of
  the second-layer split (4.17 GB), took 39 s in lrat-check and 124 s in cake_lpr.
- **Not directly comparable.** A cube here covers every parallelogram hull at once, while a P8 subcase of
  `results/C2C34` covers one of 8 canonical hull cases. Solve times of single pieces therefore do not compare the
  encodings.

**Estimate.** Closing the parallelogram case in this encoding means refuting the 196 second-layer representatives, each
split as deep as needed. The samples are too small for a reliable total, but they show where the cost lies:
- many hexagonal second layers are refuted directly, and hard hexagonal nodes split cheaply;
- Q3.11 has 288 third-layer children, 24 of them quadrilateral, and none of the 4 of those run was refuted within
  1,200 s;
- in Q3.11/Q4.30, all 20 quadrilateral fourth-layer children gave no answer within 120 s and were split again.

The cost is dominated by these chains of nested quadrilaterals.
