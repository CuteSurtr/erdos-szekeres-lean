\begin{abstract}
Let $ES(7)$ be the least $n$ such that every $n$ points in general position in the plane contain $7$ points in convex
position; the Erd\H{o}s--Szekeres conjecture predicts $ES(7)=33$. We show, with computer-assisted proofs, that every set
of at least $33$ points in general position that is mapped into itself by an isometry or an affine bijection other than
the identity contains a convex heptagon. The bound is sharp: there are centrally symmetric and mirror-symmetric
$32$-point sets without a convex heptagon. The theorem reduces to four cases: a half-turn and $34$ points, a threefold
rotation and $33$ points, a fivefold rotation and $35$ points, and a reflection; this reduction is checked in Lean~4.
The mirror case follows from Baek's theorem on the case $a=4$ of the Erd\H{o}s--Tuza--Valtr conjecture through an
elementary reduction, which is also checked in Lean with a formalization of Baek's theorem; we confirm it independently
with SAT certificates. The fivefold case also follows from a computation reported by Dumitru. The threefold and central
cases rest on SAT case splits with formally checked bookkeeping, written soundness proofs of the encodings, proof
certificates accepted by two checkers and, for most pieces, verdicts recorded when the proofs were checked. We also
record the largest heptagon-free sets for each symmetry type, with their antecedents. None of this settles $ES(7)$ or
improves its upper bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Erd\H{o}s and Szekeres~\cite{ES35} proved that $ES(k)$ is finite and constructed $2^{k-2}$ points in general position with
no $k$ in convex position~\cite{ES61}; they conjectured $ES(k)=2^{k-2}+1$. The conjecture holds for $k\le 6$, the case
$k=6$ by a computer search of Szekeres and Peters~\cite{SP06}, and $ES(k)\le 2^{k+o(k)}$ by Suk~\cite{Suk17}. The first
open case is $k=7$.

Symmetric configurations are a natural restricted search space. Subercaseaux, Mackey, Qian and Heule~\cite{SMQH25}
built rotational symmetry into SAT encodings, found symmetric extremal sets for $k=6$, reported that no real fourfold
symmetric $32$-point set avoids a convex heptagon, asked for a realized twofold symmetric $32$-point example, and
suspected that symmetric constructions exist for all $k\ge7$. Here we show, with computer-assisted proofs, that there is
no symmetric counterexample to $ES(7)=33$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}); its mirror case is implied by a theorem of
Baek~\cite{Bae25}, and its fivefold case also by a computation reported by Dumitru~\cite{Dum25}. Heptagon-free
$32$-point sets exist with a half-turn and with a reflection, while every set of $32$ or more points with a rotation of
order at least $3$ contains a convex heptagon (Proposition~\ref{prop:C}).
Other recent computational work on $ES(7)$~\cite{Dum25,KPH26,ZLRO26,Sch26,GGTW25} does not treat symmetric sets;
Koshelev and Koshka~\cite{KK26} treat empty hexagons under axial symmetry, a different problem.

Throughout, \emph{general position} means no three points on a line, and a \emph{$k$-gon} is a set of $k$ points in convex
position. Computations are described with their trust base in Section~\ref{sec:trust}; directory names refer to
\texttt{cloud/es7-2026-10-19/results} in the repository. Statements that no prior result was found refer to the sources
of Section~\ref{sec:lit} only.

\section{Which symmetries are possible}
The symmetry group of a finite set fixes its centroid, so it is a cyclic group $C_s$ of rotations or a dihedral group.
For a rotation of order $s$ about $c$, every orbit other than $\{c\}$ has exactly $s$ points and is the vertex set of a
regular $s$-gon.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sizes}
Let $S$ be in general position and invariant under a rotation $\rho$ of order $s\ge 2$ about $c$.
(a) If $s\ge 7$ and $S\ne\{c\}$, then $S$ contains a $7$-gon.
(b) If $s$ is even and $|S|\ge 3$, then $c\notin S$ and $s$ divides $|S|$.
(c) If $s$ is odd, then $|S|\equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) An orbit is a convex $s$-gon. (b) $\rho^{s/2}$ is the half-turn, so $c,p,\rho^{s/2}(p)$ are collinear for every $p$;
hence $c\notin S$ and all orbits have size $s$. (c) At most the centre is a fixed point.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:main}
Every set of at least $33$ points in general position in the plane that is mapped into itself by an isometry or an affine
bijection other than the identity contains $7$ points in convex position.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $g$ be such a map and $S$ the set. Since $g$ permutes $S$ and $S$ spans the plane, $g$ has finite order. An affine
map of finite order becomes an isometry after a linear change of coordinates (average an inner product over the group
it generates), and such a change preserves general position and convex position; so let $g$ be an isometry. Being of
finite order, $g$ is a reflection or a rotation. Reflections are handled by Theorem~\ref{thm:M}. Otherwise a power of
$g$ is the half-turn or a rotation of prime order $p$. For the half-turn, $c\notin S$ and $|S|$ is even
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(b)), hence at least $34$, and $17$ antipodal pairs form a set excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:C2}.
For $p\ge7$ use Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(a). For $p=3$ or $5$, delete the centre if it is in $S$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(c) at least $33$, respectively $35$, points remain, and $11$, respectively $7$, whole orbits form a
set excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, respectively Theorem~\ref{thm:E}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Lean]\label{rem:lean}
The deduction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from five case statements, the cases of Theorems~\ref{thm:C2}, \ref{thm:A},
\ref{thm:E}, \ref{thm:M33} and~\ref{thm:M34}, is checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib, for isometries and for affine bijections
(\texttt{SYMLEAN}; axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}). The case statements are
hypotheses there. A further file (\texttt{SYMLEAN/mirror}) proves the two mirror statements in Lean from the
repository's formalization of Baek's theorem (Section~\ref{sec:mirror}), with the same axioms and no SAT input, so that
only the half-turn, threefold and fivefold statements remain hypotheses; an independent check recompiled it. No step
from a CNF formula to a case statement is checked in Lean.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:only}
Assume Theorems~\ref{thm:A}, \ref{thm:M} and~\ref{thm:E} and the fourfold $32$-point exclusion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:C}, but not Theorem~\ref{thm:C2}. Then a set of at least $33$ points in general position without a
convex heptagon either has no nontrivial symmetry or has symmetry group exactly the half-turn group, and then its centre
is not a point of the set and its size is even, at least $34$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; a rotation of order $4$ has $8$ whole orbits excluded by the fourfold
exclusion, and order $6$ contains order $3$. The half-turn remains.
\end{proof}

The threefold and fivefold encodings below need a centre in general position with respect to the set.
\begin{lemma}[centre perturbation]\label{lem:perturb}
Let $m\ge3$ be odd and let $S$ be in general position and invariant under the rotation $R$ of order $m$ about $c\notin S$.
Rotating each orbit about $c$ by its own small angle gives an $R$-invariant set in general position whose triples have the
same orientations as those of $S$; for generic angles no line through $c$ contains two of its points.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Rotations about $c$ commute with $R$, so invariance is kept, and the orientations of triples of $S$ are nonzero and depend
continuously on the angles. Two points of one orbit are never collinear with $c$, since $m$ is odd. For points of two
different orbits, collinearity with $c$ fixes the difference of the two angles modulo $\pi$, which generic angles avoid.
\end{proof}

\section{Threefold symmetry}
\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:A}
Every set of $33$ points in general position that is invariant under a rotation of order $3$ contains $7$ points in convex
position. The largest threefold-symmetric set in general position without a convex heptagon has exactly $31$ points.
\end{theorem}
A threefold set has $3m$ or $3m+1$ points, so one with at least $33$ points contains $11$ whole orbits of size $3$, a
$33$-point threefold set without its centre; $32$ is not a possible size. An exact $31$-point example (centre included)
is \texttt{SYMMAX/c3\_31points.txt}.

For the $33$-point case, a projective normal form of the labelling (\texttt{C3-S/soundness.md}), with a generic centre by
Lemma~\ref{lem:perturb}, gives a CNF with $168{,}399$ variables and $4{,}154{,}921$ clauses. Its unsatisfiability was
established by splitting on the convex layers and on orientation bits of symmetric residues into $116{,}883$ certified
pieces. A reflection of the $H6$ strata (Lemma H6M) leaves $910$ depth-$2$ roots. For each piece, CaDiCaL produced an LRAT
proof that was accepted at solve time both by \texttt{lrat-check} and by the formally verified checker
\texttt{cake\_lpr}~\cite{THM21}; these proofs were then deleted. Two independently written composers recompute the CNF
hash of every certified piece from the base formula and find all $910$ roots covered (\texttt{C3FINAL}); that the
$73{,}567$ leaves of one explicit cut cover all $910$ roots is checked in Lean (\texttt{C3LEAN}, core Lean,
\texttt{decide +kernel}). In addition, $2{,}120$ randomly drawn pieces were re-solved from scratch with fresh proofs and
both checkers (\texttt{C3SPOT}). That draw excluded the pieces recorded in $\sigma$-cube format; a separate run on
1 November re-solved the $204$ of them that the cover cannot do without, on CNFs with the recorded hashes and with both
checkers: all $204$ were certified, and their proofs are archived (\texttt{C3PIECES}).

The closest earlier results concern other parameters: for $16$ points without a convex hexagon the threefold formula is
unsatisfiable~\cite[\S6.1]{SMQH25},~\cite{Mac25}, and a heptagon-free set of $32$ points can only have $1$-, $2$- or
$4$-fold rotational symmetry~\cite[\S6.2]{SMQH25}. Dumitru~\cite[Table~1]{Dum25} reports unsatisfiable runs for two
convex-layer templates compatible with threefold symmetry; a threefold $33$-point counterexample may have any of $144$
layer profiles, so these runs do not imply Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\section{Mirror symmetry}\label{sec:mirror}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:M}
Every mirror-symmetric set of at least $33$ points in general position contains $7$ points in convex position. The largest
mirror-symmetric set in general position without a convex heptagon has exactly $32$ points.
\end{theorem}
The upper bound follows from a theorem of Baek by an elementary reduction, which we give first;
Theorems~\ref{thm:M33} and~\ref{thm:M34} below are independent computer proofs. For a set with distinct
$x$-coordinates, an \emph{$a$-cap} (an \emph{$a$-cup}) is a sequence of $a$ of its points with increasing $x$ whose
consecutive slopes decrease (increase).

\begin{theorem}[Baek~{\cite[Thm.~1.6]{Bae25}}]\label{thm:baek}
For $n\ge4$, every set of $\binom{n-1}{2}+2$ points in general position with distinct $x$-coordinates contains a $4$-cap
or $n$ points in convex position.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[mirror lemma]\label{lem:mirror}
Let $\mu$ be the reflection in the $x$-axis and let $c_1,\dots,c_m$ $(m\ge2)$ be a cap with $y(c_1),y(c_m)\ge0$. Then
$y(c_i)>0$ for $1<i<m$, and $C\cup\mu C$, where $C=\{c_1,\dots,c_m\}$, is in convex position; it has $2m$ points, minus
one for each endpoint on the axis.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An interior point of a cap lies strictly above the chord of its neighbours, which have height $\ge0$. Let $f$ be the
piecewise linear interpolation of the $c_i$ on $I=[x(c_1),x(c_m)]$. It is concave, with a strict decrease of slope at
each $x(c_i)$, $1<i<m$, and $f\ge0$. The region $Q=\{(x,y):x\in I,\ -f(x)\le y\le f(x)\}$ is convex, as the intersection
of a strip, the hypograph of $f$ and the epigraph of $-f$. Its boundary is the polygon $c_1\dots c_m\,\mu c_m\dots\mu c_1$
(a repeated point deleted at an end on the axis), which is $x$-monotone, hence simple. Every point of $C\cup\mu C$ is an
extreme point of $Q$: the points over $x(c_1)$ and $x(c_m)$ are the endpoints of the vertical faces of $Q$ there, and
$c_i$ for $1<i<m$ is not the midpoint of two points of $Q$, by strict concavity of $f$ at $x(c_i)$; symmetrically for
$\mu c_i$. So $C\cup\mu C$ is in convex position.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[mirror reduction]\label{prop:mirrorred}
Let $j\ge2$, $k=2j+1$, and suppose that every set in general position with distinct $x$-coordinates, with no $(j+1)$-cap
and no $k$ points in convex position, has at most $M$ points. Then every mirror-symmetric set in general position with no
$k$ points in convex position has at most $2M$ points.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the mirror as the $x$-axis and let $R$ and $A$ be the points of $S$ above it and on it. Then $|S|=2|R|+|A|$ and
$|A|\le2$. For $a\in A$ the points of $R\cup\{a\}$ have distinct $x$-coordinates: if $p\ne q$ had the same one, $p$, $q$
and $\mu p$ (or $\mu q$) would be three collinear points of $S$. A $(j+1)$-cap of $R$ would give $2j+2$ points in convex
position by Lemma~\ref{lem:mirror}. A $(j+1)$-cap of $R\cup\{a\}$ through $a$ has $a$ as an endpoint, by the first claim
of Lemma~\ref{lem:mirror}, and gives $2j+1=k$ points in convex position. So $|R|\le M$, and $|R|\le M-1$ if $A$ is not
empty; in both cases $|S|\le 2M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:M}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:baek} with $n=7$ gives $M=\binom{6}{2}+1=16$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:mirrorred} for $j=3$, so a
mirror-symmetric heptagon-free set has at most $32$ points. An exact $32$-point example with $16$ pairs and no point on
the axis is \texttt{SYMMAX/mirror\_32points.txt}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:mirrorcredit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:M}, including the cases of Theorems~\ref{thm:M33} and~\ref{thm:M34}, is therefore a consequence of
Baek's theorem. The case $n=7$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:baek} had been verified by SAT for pseudolinear colorings by Balko
and Valtr~\cite{BV15}, and it has its own LRAT certificate here ($17$ points, accepted by \texttt{lrat-check} only;
\texttt{MIRRORETV}). Proposition~\ref{prop:mirrorred} and the bound $32$ are checked in Lean~4 for a
reflection in an arbitrary line, with the repository's formalization of Baek's theorem (adapted from Baek's code, with
bridge lemmas adapted from Schroeder's archive~\cite{Sch26}) and no SAT input (\texttt{SYMLEAN/mirror}). A
mirror-symmetric $32$-point set also follows from the classical construction in Lov\'asz's form~\cite[Thm.~2.6]{MS00},
with the cluster at angle $-\theta$ chosen as the mirror image of the cluster at $\theta$; for $k=7$ no cluster lies on
the axis. Our example was found independently by search. No matching prior result was found for
Proposition~\ref{prop:mirrorred} or for its combination with Baek's theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:M33}
A mirror-symmetric set of $33$ points in general position with exactly one point on the axis contains a $7$-gon.
\end{theorem}
\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:M34}
A mirror-symmetric set of $34$ points in general position with no point on the axis contains a $7$-gon.
\end{theorem}
These give a second proof of the upper bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:M}, independent of Baek's theorem. By general position at
most two points lie on the axis. If none does, the size is even, hence at least $34$, and $17$ mirror pairs form an
axis-free $34$-point mirror set. If one or two do, $16$ mirror pairs and one axis point form a $33$-point mirror set with
exactly one point on the axis. Theorem~\ref{thm:M33} has a further proof (\texttt{WRITEUP/MIRROR.md}) by a signotope
encoding whose soundness, normal forms, case split and leaf cover are verified in Lean~4; its roughly $75{,}000$ CNF facts
carry recorded \texttt{cake\_lpr} verdicts, and their proof files were not retained. The proofs of
Theorems~\ref{thm:M33} and~\ref{thm:M34} by the encoding below are single CNF formulas with retained LRAT proofs accepted
by \texttt{lrat-check} and \texttt{cake\_lpr} (\texttt{SYMZ}).

\paragraph{The encoding.} Put the axis on the $y$-axis, write the pairs as $L_i=(-a_i,b_i)$, $R_i=(a_i,b_i)$ with
$a_i>0$, use homogeneous vectors $v_p=(1,x_p,y_p)$, and add one element $Z$ with vector $(0,1,0)$, the horizontal point
at infinity. The chirotope $\chi=\operatorname{sign}\det$ of the set and $Z$ satisfies:
(i) $\det(v_Z,v_p,v_q)=y_p-y_q$, so the zero triples are exactly $\{Z,L_i,R_i\}$;
(ii) the reflection acts by $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)$, which has determinant $-1$ and negates $v_Z$, so it reverses
the orientation of triples of set points and preserves that of triples containing $Z$;
(iii) $v_{R_i}-v_{L_i}=2a_iv_Z$ with $a_i>0$, so $\chi(Z,L_i,q)=\chi(R_i,L_i,q)=\chi(Z,R_i,q)$;
(iv) the three-term Grassmann--Pl\"ucker relations hold, and the vectors are acyclic, since $w+\varepsilon x$ is positive
on all of them for small $\varepsilon>0$;
(v) numbering the pairs by decreasing height gives $\det(v_Z,v_{L_i},v_{L_j})=b_i-b_j>0$ for $i<j$.
The CNF encodes (i)--(v), with variables for the classes of identified triples, and the absence of a $7$-gon through the
four-point criterion. Every real configuration therefore gives a satisfying assignment, so unsatisfiability excludes
all real configurations. The converse fails: a satisfying assignment need not be realizable, and $Z$ imposes no metric
condition. For $34$ points the CNF has $838{,}032$ variables and $16{,}642{,}456$ clauses and is refuted by CaDiCaL in
$443$\,s; for $33$ points with one axis point it has $695{,}928$ variables and $14{,}080{,}656$ clauses and is refuted in
$964$\,s. The encoding was tested on real symmetric sets and on $3{,}300$ random symmetric sets (satisfiable exactly when
no $K$-gon exists, no mismatch), and it reproduces the mirror hexagon bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:mirror}.

\section{Central symmetry}\label{sec:central}
\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:C2}
Every centrally symmetric set of $34$ points in general position contains $7$ points in convex position. Hence every
centrally symmetric set of at least $34$ points does, and the largest centrally symmetric set in general position without
a convex heptagon has exactly $32$ points.
\end{theorem}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(b) the centre $c$ is not a point of such a set, so the set consists of antipodal pairs, and
deleting pairs gives the second statement; a $32$-point example is Proposition~\ref{prop:B}.

\paragraph{The encoding.} The centre is an element: $v_c=(v_p+v_{-p})/2$ gives $\chi(c,p,q)=\chi(-p,p,q)$; the half-turn
acts by a matrix of determinant $+1$ that fixes $v_c$, so it preserves all orientations; and by general position the zero
triples are exactly $\{c,p,-p\}$, so no perturbation of the centre is needed. The first point is a vertex of the convex
hull, and the other pairs are represented by their point to the left of the line from $c$ to the first point, sorted by
angle about $c$. The base CNF (\texttt{SYMZ/zcnf.py rot 17 7 --m 2 --hull}) has $838{,}032$ variables and
$16{,}653{,}336$ clauses. A written proof, refereed independently (sound, with minor gaps in the presentation), shows that
every centrally symmetric $34$-point set in general position without a convex heptagon, labelled in this normal form from
any hull vertex, satisfies every clause (\texttt{C2ENC}). Consequently, if $Q,Q_1,\dots,Q_n$ are cubes
(sets of literals) and the base with the units of $Q$ and the clauses $\neg Q_1,\dots,\neg Q_n$ is unsatisfiable, then
every labelled real configuration satisfying $Q$ satisfies some $Q\wedge Q_i$; dropping the literals of $Q$ from each
$\neg Q_i$ is equivalent under the units of $Q$. This is how the covers below are used.

\paragraph{The case split.} The convex hull is a centrally symmetric polygon with $4$ or $6$ vertices: $8$ vertices would
contain a convex heptagon. In the normal form it is determined by one further pair (a parallelogram $P_a$, $16$ cases) or
two (a hexagon, $120$ cases). The base with the negations of all $136$ hull cubes is unsatisfiable, and all $120$
hexagonal cases are unsatisfiable, each with a retained proof accepted by both checkers. Restarting the normal form at the
other hull class of a parallelogram maps $P_a$ to $P_{17-a}$ (reduction A), so $P_1,\dots,P_8$ suffice. Each $P_a$ is
split by the convex hull of the $30$ inner points, a centrally symmetric quadrilateral or hexagon, into
$\binom{15}{2}+\binom{15}{3}=560$ subcases, with a certified cover relative to the hull cube of $P_a$. Reflecting the
configuration and restarting the normal form maps class $i$ to $(a-i)\bmod 17$ (reduction B) and pairs the subcases into
$287$ orbits, $14$ of them fixed. Reductions A and B have written proofs, reviewed independently, and were also checked
on the formula: each relabelling maps every base clause into the base together with the hull clauses of the case.

\paragraph{Closure.} The $8$ covers and the $8\times287$ orbits were closed with $2{,}492$ solver runs: $2{,}385$
unsatisfiable, $107$ without an answer within the time limit, none satisfiable. Each unsatisfiable run produced an LRAT
proof that was accepted by \texttt{lrat-check} and \texttt{cake\_lpr} and then deleted; its proof hash, both verdicts and,
except for seven pilot units, its CNF hash were recorded. All $2{,}589$ recorded CNF hashes were rebuilt from the base,
and that every orbit of every case has a closed member is checked in Lean (core Lean, \texttt{decide +kernel};
\texttt{C2FINAL}). Seven pilot units without a recorded hash were re-solved. A random sample of $24$ units recorded on the
cloud machines, drawn before any result was seen, was re-solved: all $24$ rebuild the recorded CNF hash and both checkers
accept the re-solved proofs; $23$ proofs are byte-identical to the recorded ones, and one is a different proof. A second
draw of $8$ units was reproduced byte for byte. Further re-checks, started on 1 November
(\texttt{C2RECHECK}): the $120$ hexagonal proofs on hash-checked files with both checkers ($120$ of $120$ accepted), the
$58$ pilot orbits of $P_8$ ($58$ of $58$ certified, all byte-identical to the recorded proofs), and the $60$ orbit
representatives and $5$ covers recorded only on the local machine ($44$ of $65$ certified by 1~November 2026, at
19:43~UTC, all byte-identical; still running). A spot check of one unit from each of the $242$ lanes not already reached by a re-solve had
$81$ units certified by the same time. Then $224$ of the $2{,}304$ orbit and cover facts ($9.7\%$) had a re-solve on a hash-checked CNF with
both checkers.

No matching prior result was found for Theorem~\ref{thm:C2}. Subercaseaux et al.~\cite{SMQH25} examined only fourfold
symmetry at $32$ points and listed a twofold $32$-point realization as open; Krapivin, Przybocki and Heule~\cite{KPH26}
found no realization among $200{,}000$ abstract $32$-point solutions, with no symmetry imposed.

\section{Companion results}\label{sec:comp}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:layers}
Let $S$ be in general position and invariant under a rotation $\rho$ of order $m\ge 4$ about a point $c\notin S$, and
suppose $S$ has no $K$ points in convex position for some $K\le 2m$. Then every convex layer of $S$ is a single orbit, and
$c$ lies strictly inside each layer.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The vertex set $V$ of $\operatorname{conv}(S)$ is invariant under $\rho$, so it is a union of orbits of $m$ points each,
and it is in convex position; hence $|V|<K\le 2m$ and $V$ is one orbit, a regular $m$-gon with centre $c$. Apply the same
argument to $S\setminus V$.
\end{proof}
A special case is stated in~\cite[\S3.2]{SMQH25}: a fourfold $16$-point set without a convex hexagon has four layers of
four points.

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:E}
Every set of at least $35$ points in general position with a rotation of order $5$ contains $7$ points in convex position.
\end{theorem}
Such a set contains a $35$-point fivefold set without centre, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:layers} would be seven nested
regular pentagons. The CNF of \texttt{SYM5/symcnf.py} for this case (an auxiliary centre, generic by
Lemma~\ref{lem:perturb}) is refuted in $415$\,s, and a second normal form of the same encoder using
Lemma~\ref{lem:layers} in $54$\,s; both LRAT proofs are retained and accepted by both checkers (\texttt{SYM5}).
\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:E} also follows from an earlier reported computation, which has priority. Dumitru~\cite[\S7,
Table~1]{Dum25} reports an unsatisfiable Kissat run (row $5^6$, $H=30$, baseline) for $33$ points whose first six convex
layers are pentagons, with the remaining three points inside them; his public generator, with zero offsets, adds to the
base encoding only within-layer convexity and nesting. Deleting two points of the innermost pentagon of a $35$-point
fivefold set gives such a configuration. No proof files or logs for that run are public, so we treat it as a prior
reported claim rather than an independently verified theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:C}
Let $N(\Gamma)$ be the largest size of a set in general position with symmetry $\Gamma$ and no $7$ points in convex
position. Then $N(C_2)=32$, $N(C_3)=31$, $N(C_4)=28$, $N(C_5)=31$, $N(C_6)=30$ and $N(\text{mirror})=32$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The examples in \texttt{SYMMAX} were found by simulated annealing with exact arithmetic, and the example in \texttt{SYM2}
is the construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:B}; all were checked by a dynamic program and by brute force over all
$7$-subsets. The upper bounds for $C_2$, $C_3$ and mirror symmetry are Theorems~\ref{thm:C2}, \ref{thm:A}
and~\ref{thm:M}. A sixfold set of $36$ points contains a threefold $33$-point set, so $N(C_6)=30$. The fivefold bound is
Theorem~\ref{thm:E} and Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(c). By Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}(b), $N(C_4)\le28$ is the statement that no
fourfold $32$-point set avoids a convex heptagon. It was stated in~\cite[\S6.2]{SMQH25}, after they found that all
solutions of their model have one of six inner $12$-point patterns; the arXiv version does not say how these patterns
were shown to be unrealizable. They also report realizable $28$-point restrictions. It is certified in
\texttt{C4CUT}: the layered fourfold formula (Lemma~\ref{lem:layers}) with six realizability cuts, each justified by a
Grassmann--Pl\"ucker final-polynomial certificate on the inner $12$ points verified in exact arithmetic, has an LRAT proof
accepted by both checkers.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An explicit centrally symmetric example.} Baek and Balko~\cite[Definition~13, Lemma~14 and \S6.1]{BB25}
describe a construction of Valtr that blows up an arbitrary set of $k-1$ points in general position into $2^{k-2}$ points
without a convex $k$-gon, using clusters with cup and cap parameters $x_i=i-1$ and $y_i=k-i-1$. For odd $k$, a
centrally symmetric base with clusters paired by the half-turn, which exchanges cups and caps and the two parameters,
gives a centrally symmetric set. The following is an explicit case of this, rotated by a quarter-turn, with a direct
proof. Sort points by $x$-coordinate. An \emph{$a$-cup} is a sequence of $a$ points whose consecutive slopes increase,
an \emph{$a$-cap} one whose slopes decrease; let $G(a,b)$ be the classical set of $\binom{a+b-4}{a-2}$ points in general
position with no $a$-cup and no $b$-cap~\cite{ES35}. The half-turn $h(p)=-p$ exchanges cups and caps.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:B}
Let $k\ge3$ be odd, $n=k-2$ and $x_i=2i-n$. For $i<n/2$ place a copy of $G(i+2,k-i)$, flattened by
$(x,y)\mapsto(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon^2y)$, at $(x_i,x_i^3)$, and for $i>n/2$ place the image under $h$ of the copy at
index $n-i$. For small $\varepsilon$ the union $S$ is a centrally symmetric set of $2^{k-2}$ points in general position
without $k$ points in convex position.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cluster $B_i$ has no $(i+2)$-cup and no $(k-i)$-cap, and $|S|=\sum_i\binom{n}{i}=2^{k-2}$. Three points of the cubic at
$x=a,b,c$ are collinear iff $a+b+c=0$, impossible for odd numbers. The slope between clusters $a<b$ is close to
$\sigma(a,b)=x_a^2+x_ax_b+x_b^2\ge1$, slopes inside clusters tend to $0$, and
$\sigma(b,c)-\sigma(a,b)=(x_c-x_a)(x_a+x_b+x_c)$; so for small $\varepsilon$ the set is in general position and
consecutive segments between clusters turn according to the sign of $x_a+x_b+x_c$.

Let $P\subseteq S$ be in convex position with leftmost point in $B_l$ and rightmost point in $B_r$. If $l=r$ then
$|P|\le k-2$. Otherwise the lower chain is a cup in $B_l$ (at most $l+1$ points), then single points in clusters
$c_1<\dots<c_t$ strictly between $l$ and $r$, and the upper chain is single points in clusters $d_1<\dots<d_s$ strictly
between, then a cap in $B_r$ (at most $k-r-1$ points). The turn conditions reduce to $x_l+x_{c_1}+x_{c_2}>0$ (with
$c_2=r$ if $t=1$) and $x_{d_{s-1}}+x_{d_s}+x_r<0$ (with $d_{s-1}=l$ if $s=1$). If $t,s\ge1$ and $c_1\le d_s$, comparing
the two sums termwise gives a contradiction; so the clusters used by the two chains are distinct, $t+s\le r-l-1$, and
$|P|\le(l+1)+(k-r-1)+t+s\le k-1$.
\end{proof}
For $k=7$ this gives $32$ points; an integer instance with coordinates of absolute value at most $1{,}250{,}000$ is in
\texttt{SYM2}, checked by a dynamic program and by brute force over all $\binom{32}{7}$ subsets. It answers the concrete
realization task of~\cite{SMQH25}; it is a special case of the construction of~\cite{BB25}, not a different general
method.

For even $k$ the cubic construction needs a cluster at the centre, and with a centre cluster of the same scale it fails.
For $k=6$ the fourfold $16$-point sets of~\cite[App.~C]{SMQH25} and~\cite[Fig.~3.28]{Mac25} are centrally symmetric. For
even $k\ge8$, centrally symmetric sets of $2^{k-2}$ points without a convex $k$-gon are implied by~\cite[Lemma~14]{BB25},
applied to either of these sets with a half-turn-symmetric labelling and clusters paired by the half-turn; neither paper
states this, and the proof of that lemma is omitted in~\cite{BB25}. Exact instances from this route with $64$ points
($k=8$) and $256$ points ($k=10$) were checked (\texttt{SYMEVEN}). The same directory gives a
self-contained written proof, refereed, of a cubic construction with a much narrower middle cluster, with exact
instances for $k=6$, $8$ and $10$.

\paragraph{Hexagons.} For $k=6$ the largest symmetric sets without a convex hexagon have $16$ points for twofold,
fourfold and fivefold rotations (the fourfold and fivefold examples are in~\cite{SMQH25}; a fourfold set is twofold),
$13$ points for threefold rotations ($15$ and $16$ points are excluded by retained LRAT proofs; $16$ agrees
with~\cite{SMQH25}), and $12$ points for a reflection, by the following elementary bound.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mirror}
Let $k=2j$ be even, and let $S$ be a mirror-symmetric set in general position with no $k$ points in convex position. Then
$|S|\le 2\binom{3j-4}{2j-2}+2$; in particular $|S|\le 12$ for $k=6$ and $|S|\le 58<2^{6}$ for $k=8$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With the mirror as the $x$-axis, the points above it have distinct $x$-coordinates. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mirror} they have no
$j$-cap; they have no $k$-cup, since a cup is in convex position. By the cups-caps theorem~\cite{ES35} there are at most
$\binom{3j-4}{2j-2}$ of them, and at most two points lie on the mirror.
\end{proof}
For $k=8$, Theorem~\ref{thm:baek} with $n=8$ improves the bound to $2\cdot22+2=46$, a corollary of Baek's theorem; this
bound is checked in Lean (\texttt{SYMLEAN/mirror}), and a $46$-point example is in \texttt{MIRRORETV}.

\section{Trust base}\label{sec:trust}
\emph{Lean-checked} are: the deduction of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from the five case statements, for isometries and affine
bijections (Remark~\ref{rem:lean}); the bookkeeping of the threefold and central case splits, that is, that the listed
leaves cover all $910$ threefold roots and that every central orbit has a listed member; and the first proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:M33}, conditional on its CNF facts. Whether a listed leaf is certified is checked outside Lean, by
recomputing its CNF hash from the base formula. The bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:M} from the formalization of Baek's
theorem, and with it the two mirror case statements, are Lean-checked as well (\texttt{SYMLEAN/mirror}).

\emph{Written proofs}, audited or refereed independently but not formalized, establish the soundness of every encoding
(threefold, fivefold and fourfold, mirror, central), Lemmas~\ref{lem:perturb} and~\ref{lem:layers}, and the normal
forms and reductions of the case splits. Lemma~\ref{lem:mirror} and Proposition~\ref{prop:mirrorred} are written proofs
that are also Lean-checked. Every case except mirror symmetry rests on one encoder; Theorem~\ref{thm:E} uses two normal
forms of one encoder.

\emph{Certificates} are LRAT proofs checked by \texttt{lrat-check} (with a patch for a known upstream defect) and by
\texttt{cake\_lpr}, a checker verified in CakeML~\cite{THM21}; the checkers are trusted, the solver is not. Retained and
accepted by both checkers are the proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:M33}, \ref{thm:M34} and~\ref{thm:E}, of the central hull
cover and the $120$ hexagonal hulls, of the fourfold $32$-point exclusion, and of the re-solved central units; the
$204$ threefold pieces re-solved on 1 November are archived as well. The $17$-point case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:baek} was accepted by \texttt{lrat-check} only. A replay of the retained certificates with logs bound to
the input hashes accepted four of them with both checkers (one of the two proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:E}, the fourfold
proof and two threefold hexagon proofs); by 1~November 2026, at 19:43~UTC, it had not been run on the mirror proofs,
the other proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:E} or the hull cover. The hull cover was replayed with both checkers later the same day (20:10~UTC).

\emph{Recorded verdicts}: for the threefold pieces and most central parallelogram pieces, both checkers accepted the proof
at solve time, and the proof was then deleted; only the CNF hash, the proof size, the proof hash (for every central
record and for $26{,}436$ threefold records) and the verdicts remain. The roughly $75{,}000$ CNF facts of the first proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:M33} carry recorded \texttt{cake\_lpr} verdicts only. These records were re-checked by random
samples and targeted re-solves, not exhaustively.

\section{What remains open}
$ES(7)$ itself is open, and no unrestricted bound is improved here. The recorded verdicts of the threefold and central
case splits can be re-checked only by re-solving the pieces, at a cost comparable to the original computations. No step
from a CNF formula to a case statement is formalized. The encodings are abstract: the centre or point at infinity as an
element imposes no metric condition, and a satisfying assignment need not be realizable. For example, the layered
fourfold $32$-point formula is satisfiable, as~\cite{SMQH25} also observed for its own model, although no real set
exists. Only unsatisfiable answers, with sound encodings and certified covers, are used.
